Providing liquidity earns you a share of every swap fee the pool collects. On
Spreadless you do it with **one** asset: no pairing, no manual split (see
[Single-Sided Liquidity](/docs/concepts/single-sided) for why that's possible).

<Figure
  alt="The deposit panel: a single-asset amount field, projected APY and estimated returns"
  caption="Depositing a single asset, with projected APY and LP received shown before you sign."
/>

## Steps

1. Open **[Earn](/earn)** and choose a pool.
2. On the **Deposit** tab, pick the asset you want to contribute and enter an
   amount (the **25 / 50 / Max** shortcuts pull from your balance).
3. Review the projected **APY** and the **LP shares** you'll receive, both quoted
   from a live simulation of the real deposit.
4. Click deposit and approve in your wallet.

## What you get back

You receive **`SLP`** LP shares (9 decimals) representing your fractional claim on
the pool. Those shares accrue [fees](/docs/concepts/fees-yield) continuously and
can be redeemed for any single asset in the pool when you
[withdraw](/docs/guides/withdraw).

<Callout kind="tip" title="Which asset to deposit">
  Because a deposit walks the same curve as a trade, adding the asset a pool is
  *short* of is cheaper, occasionally even rewarded, while adding the asset it's
  already heavy in costs a little. The pool's composition bar shows the current
  balance.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="note" title="Slippage protection on deposits too">
  The deposit submits with a `min_lp_out` floor derived from your quote minus
  tolerance. If the pool moves and can't mint at least that many shares, the
  contract reverts. You're never given fewer shares than you accepted. See
  [Slippage & Safety](/docs/protocol/slippage-and-safety).
</Callout>

<Callout kind="warning" title="The main risk is a de-peg">
  Your position's value rests on the pool's assets holding their peg. If one
  de-pegs, the pool accumulates the weak asset and your redeemable value shifts
  toward it. This is the stablecoin form of impermanent loss, which
  [Fees & Yield](/docs/concepts/fees-yield) covers.
</Callout>
