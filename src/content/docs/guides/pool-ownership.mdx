Every pool has an owner, and it is the wallet that created it. Ownership is a
narrower role than the name suggests: the owner sets the swap fee, the per-token
reserve caps, the LP supply cap and the pause switch. That is the whole list.

## Ownership does not decide `A`

This is the part worth reading twice, because it used to work the other way.

Who may move the amplification factor `A` is a separate setting, `amp_control`,
written into the pool when it is created and immutable from that ledger on. It
is either `Locked`, meaning `A` never moves again for anyone, or
`ProtocolManaged`, meaning Spreadless may ramp it. The owner has no say either
way, and transferring the pool does not carry that right with it.

So a pool can perfectly well be owned by its creator and still have an `A` only
Spreadless can touch, or be owned by Spreadless and have an `A` nobody can touch.
The two questions are simply unrelated.

## The choice at creation

Step 3 of the pool builder, **Right to change A**, sets `amp_control` and
nothing else:

- **Flexible.** `ProtocolManaged`. If a market shifts, Spreadless can retune `A`,
  always as a slow ramp over a set duration, never as a jump.
- **Fixed.** `Locked`. `A` is frozen at launch, for everyone, Spreadless
  included.

Either way you deploy in **one signature** and you own the pool afterwards.
There is no second step and no state left half-decided.

## Handing a pool over

A pool you own shows a **Transfer** button in the Owner row of its page. You can
hand it to Spreadless, or to any other Stellar address, account (`G...`) or
contract (`C...`).

A transfer is a two-step handshake. You sign an **offer**. The recipient signs an
**accept**. Nothing moves until they do. The offer stays open for seven days and
then lapses. While it is open you can take it back, and the pool page shows the
offer with a copyable invite link. The recipient opens that link with their
wallet connected and finds the Accept step waiting.

<Callout kind="note" title="A wrong address costs nothing">
  Ownership only moves when the recipient accepts. An address nobody controls can
  never accept, so a mistyped offer simply lapses and the pool stays yours.
</Callout>

## Ownership cannot be given up

The pool contract keeps `renounce_ownership()` in its standard interface but
always refuses it. A pool can never be left without an owner; it can only be
handed to somebody else. If you want a pool you no longer run, transfer it.

If you were looking for this as the way to freeze `A`, it never was, and it is
not needed: **Fixed** at creation does exactly that and does it in the same
signature that makes the pool.

## What the states mean

| Setting | Decides | Set by | Can it change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `amp_control` | who may ramp `A` | the creator, at creation | never |
| owner | fee, caps, LP cap, pause | the creator, then whoever accepts a transfer | by transfer only |

Every offer, withdrawal and accept appears in your
[Activity](/docs/guides/portfolio) history under the **Ownership** filter.
