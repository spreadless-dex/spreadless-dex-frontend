Liquidity providers earn because **traders pay a fee on every swap**, and that fee
accrues to the pool's reserves, which LP shares have a proportional claim on. No
external emissions are required for the base return; it's the pool's own trading
activity.

## How fees accrue

Each swap leaves a small fee behind in the pool. Because your LP shares represent a
fixed fraction of the pool, and the pool's reserves grow as fees accumulate, the
value each share can redeem slowly increases. You don't claim fees as a separate
action. They compound into the reserves, and you realise them when you withdraw.

Your return therefore scales with:

- **Volume**: more trades, more fees.
- **Your share of the pool**: a bigger slice earns a bigger cut.
- **Time in the pool**: fees accrue continuously while you're providing.

## How APY is shown

Where the interface shows an APY figure (and it's the **one place the design uses
its purple accent**) it is an *annualised projection* from recent activity, not a
guaranteed or fixed rate. It moves with volume and liquidity and can fall as well
as rise.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Illustrative figures on testnet">
  TVL, volume, and APY numbers shown in the testnet app are illustrative. Several
  are oriented on the recent stats of a comparable production protocol rather than
  live on-chain data. Treat them as design placeholders, not a forecast.
</Callout>

## What eats into the return

An LP's net result is fees earned minus a few frictions:

- **Rebalancing drift.** If a pool sits imbalanced, the reserve mix shifts toward
  the weaker asset. For genuinely pegged assets this is minor; if one asset
  *de-pegs*, LPs end up holding more of the failing one. This is the stablecoin
  analogue of impermanent loss and is the main risk to the yield.
- **Deposit / withdrawal price impact.** Entering or exiting single-sided walks
  the curve and costs a little (see
  [Single-Sided Liquidity](/docs/concepts/single-sided)).
- **Network fees.** Small on Stellar, but non-zero.

<Callout kind="note" title="The real risk is the peg">
  The invariant's high amplification is what keeps trading efficient, but it also
  means a pool will keep quoting near 1:1 well into a de-peg, accumulating the
  weak asset. The health of an LP position ultimately rests on the assets in the
  pool actually holding their peg.
</Callout>
