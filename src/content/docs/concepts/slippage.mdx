**Price impact** is how much your own trade moves the price against you.
**Slippage** is the broader term for the gap between the price you expected and
the price you executed at — including price impact plus any drift between quoting
and settlement. On a stablecoin AMM, price impact is usually the dominant part,
and it comes straight out of the curve's shape.

## Where it comes from

When you swap, you add your input token to one reserve and remove the output token
from another. That shifts the pool along its invariant curve. The **slope of the
curve at your landing point is the marginal price** — and because you move the
reserves as you trade, you slide down a slightly worse slope with every unit. The
average of those slopes is your effective price; the difference from the starting
price is the price impact.

- On a **curved** (constant-product) pool, the slope changes immediately, so even
  small trades pay.
- On the **flat** middle of a StableSwap pool, the slope barely changes, so price
  impact stays tiny until the pool is pushed far from balance.

The [interactive model](/docs/concepts/invariant-curve) shows both numbers side
by side for the same trade — that gap is the point of the whole design.

## Two things that determine your slippage

1. **Trade size relative to the pool.** A trade that is small next to total
   liquidity stays on the flat section. A trade large enough to meaningfully
   rebalance the pool walks into the steep part.
2. **How balanced the pool already is.** Starting from a lopsided pool, you're
   already on a steeper part of the curve before you begin — so the same trade
   costs more.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Keep slippage low">
  Prefer pools with deep, balanced reserves for large orders, and split very large
  trades if a single one would tilt the pool. Each pool's composition bar on its
  detail page shows how balanced it currently is.
</Callout>

## How the app protects you

Spreadless never relies on the on-screen quote alone. Every swap, deposit, and
withdrawal enforces a **minimum-received floor on-chain**:

1. It **simulates** the trade to get an exact quote.
2. It subtracts your slippage tolerance to compute a hard minimum.
3. It submits the real transaction carrying that minimum. If conditions move and
   the pool can't deliver at least that amount, the contract **reverts** — you
   are never silently filled at a worse price.

The swap panel shows this explicitly as *Minimum received* under "Execution
protection". The mechanism is documented in full under
[Slippage & Safety](/docs/protocol/slippage-and-safety).

<Callout kind="note" title="Tolerance precision">
  Swap tolerance is expressed in parts-per-million, not just basis points, because
  on tightly-pegged pairs traders genuinely ask for floors below 0.01% — a
  precision basis points can't express.
</Callout>
