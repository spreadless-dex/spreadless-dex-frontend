Every AMM is governed by one rule, called an **invariant**: a relationship
between the pool's reserves that must stay constant through every trade. When you
put one token in and take another out, the invariant is what decides the exchange
rate. Understanding its *shape* is understanding the whole protocol.

## Two shapes, one picture

Drag the sliders. The solid line is the StableSwap curve Spreadless uses; the
dashed line is the constant-product curve (`x · y = k`) that a conventional AMM
like Uniswap uses. Both pools hold the same reserves and both price the same
trade — the only difference is the shape of the rule.

<CurveVisualizer client:visible />

The two curves pass through the same balanced point in the middle, but they
behave completely differently around it:

- **Constant product** (dashed) is curved *everywhere*. Even a small trade slides
  you along a bend, so you always pay some price impact — and it grows steeply.
- **StableSwap** (solid) is deliberately **flat near the middle**. Around the 1:1
  point it behaves almost like a straight line — trade in, get nearly the same
  amount out — and only curves sharply once the pool becomes badly imbalanced.

## Why "flat" is the whole point

For two assets that are both supposed to be worth a dollar, the *fair* exchange
rate is 1:1. A good stablecoin AMM should hold that rate steady across a wide
range of trade sizes and only defend itself when the pool tilts far from balance.
That is exactly what the flat middle section does:

- Near balance, moving reserves barely moves the price → **low slippage**.
- Far from balance, the curve steepens quickly → the pool gets **expensive to
  drain**, which protects the liquidity providers still holding the scarce side.

<Callout kind="math" title="The invariant, precisely">
  For a two-coin pool with reserves `x` and `y`, amplification `A`, and invariant
  total `D`, StableSwap holds:

  `A · 4 · (x + y) + D = A · D · 4 + D³ / (4 · x · y)`

  When `A → 0` this collapses to the constant-product curve `x · y = k`. When
  `A → ∞` it becomes the constant-sum line `x + y = D` — a perfectly flat 1:1
  exchange. Every real pool lives between those two extremes, and the
  [amplification factor](/docs/concepts/amplification) is the dial that places it.
</Callout>

## What sets the amount you receive

The curve is fixed the instant a trade is simulated, so the output is fully
determined — there is no hidden spread and no order book. What you receive is
simply the vertical distance you travel along the curve when you push the pool's
reserves sideways by your input amount. A flatter curve means a shorter drop,
which means more tokens out. That single geometric fact is the entire pitch.
