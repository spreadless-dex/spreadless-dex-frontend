title: Single-Sided Liquidity
description: Why you can deposit just one stablecoin into a multi-asset pool — and what you actually receive.
---

On a classic two-sided AMM you must supply *both* assets of a pair in the right
ratio. If you only hold one, you first have to sell half of it — paying fees and
price impact — just to provide liquidity. Spreadless removes that step: you
deposit **one** asset and the pool handles the rest.

## Why it works here

Single-sided deposits are only sane because the invariant already **assumes the
assets are close to parity**. The pool can accept an imbalanced contribution and
value it fairly against the curve, because near the peg all the assets are worth
almost the same. On a volatile-pair AMM the same move would be a large, lossy
swap; on a stablecoin pool near balance it's a minor, well-priced rebalance.

## What you receive: LP shares

When you deposit, the pool mints you **LP shares** — a token (`SLP`, 9 decimals)
representing your fractional claim on the pool. The pool contract *is* the LP
token: it implements the standard fungible-token interface alongside the swap
logic, so your share balance is read the same way as any token balance.

Your shares entitle you to:

- a proportional slice of the pool's reserves, and
- a proportional slice of the [trading fees](/docs/concepts/fees-yield) the pool
  earns while you're in it.

## The deposit has its own price impact

A single-sided deposit *does* nudge the pool off balance, so it carries a small
price impact of its own — exactly the same curve mechanics as a swap. Depositing
the asset a pool is already short of can even earn a small bonus; depositing the
asset it's already heavy in costs a little. The app quotes the LP you'll receive
by simulating the real deposit, so the number you see is the number the contract
computes.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Balanced pools, cheaper deposits">
  Because the deposit walks the same curve as a trade, adding the *scarcer* side
  of an imbalanced pool is cheaper (sometimes rewarded) and adding the *abundant*
  side is slightly costlier. The pool composition bar shows which is which.
</Callout>

## Exiting

You are never locked into the mix. Burning your LP shares can pay out into **any
single** supported asset in the pool via `withdraw_one_token` — so you can enter
with `USDx` and leave entirely in `PYUSD` if you want. Withdrawing one token also
walks the curve, so the same price-impact rules apply.

<Callout kind="note" title="On-chain floor here too">
  Both deposit and withdrawal enforce a minimum-out on-chain (`min_lp_out` when
  depositing, `min_amount_out` when withdrawing), so a moving pool can't fill you
  below the amount you accepted. See
  [Slippage & Safety](/docs/protocol/slippage-and-safety).
</Callout>
