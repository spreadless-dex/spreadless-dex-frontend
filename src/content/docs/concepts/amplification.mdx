The **amplification factor**, written `A`, is the one tunable number in the
StableSwap invariant. It controls how strongly the curve is pulled toward a flat,
1:1 exchange near the balance point. Everything users feel about the pool —
slippage, capital efficiency, how hard it is to drain — traces back to this
number.

## Turn the dial

Move the **A** slider and watch the solid curve. At low `A` it hugs the curved
constant-product line; as you raise `A` it flattens toward a straight 1:1
exchange, and the same trade returns visibly more.

## What A means, intuitively

Think of `A` as **how confident the pool is that the two assets are worth the
same**.

- **Low A** (near 1): the pool barely assumes correlation. It behaves like an
  ordinary constant-product AMM — safe for loosely-related assets, but with real
  price impact on every trade.
- **High A** (hundreds to low thousands): the pool assumes tight correlation. It
  keeps the price glued near 1:1 across a wide range of trade sizes — excellent
  for stablecoins that hold their peg, and very capital-efficient.

<Callout kind="math" title="Where A sits in the formula">
  For a two-coin pool: `A · 4 · (x + y) + D = A · D · 4 + D³ / (4·x·y)`.

  `A` multiplies the constant-sum term `(x + y)`. The larger it is, the more the
  invariant behaves like the flat line `x + y = D`. As `A → 0`, that term
  vanishes and you're left with constant product `x·y = k`. So `A` literally
  weights how much "flat 1:1 line" versus "curved x·y=k" the pool is.
</Callout>

## The trade-off

A higher `A` is not free. The flatter the curve near balance, the **more
violently it steepens once the pool goes off-balance**. That is by design — it
concentrates liquidity where the assets trade near parity and withdraws it fast
if one asset starts to fail — but it means a high-`A` pool punishes imbalance
harder. Choosing `A` is choosing where on that spectrum a pool should live:
maximum efficiency at peg, versus resilience when a peg breaks.

## Reading a pool's A

Every pool exposes its effective amplification on-chain via `get_amp()`, and the
front-end surfaces it on each pool's detail page under **Parameters & Health**.
The value can change over time: contracts can *ramp* `A` gradually between two
values, and `get_amp()` always returns the current effective figure, ramp
included.

<Callout kind="note" title="Effective, not nominal">
  If a pool is mid-ramp, the `A` you read is the interpolated value for the
  current ledger — the number the curve is actually being priced with right now,
  not the start or target.
</Callout>
