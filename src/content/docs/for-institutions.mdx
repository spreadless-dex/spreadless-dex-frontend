Most decentralized exchanges are optimised for speculation on volatile,
uncorrelated pairs. If your job is moving stablecoins predictably, whether you
are a trading desk rebalancing inventory, a treasury settling in a different
unit, or a fintech routing payouts, that machinery works against you. Spreadless is built for the
other case.

## The problem, in one sentence

> Every trade has two prices: the quote you see and the amount you receive.

On a constant-product AMM those two numbers diverge quickly with size, and the
divergence is hard to predict before you commit. For a desk clearing six or seven
figures of stablecoins, that unpredictability *is* the cost.

## What Spreadless does about it

| Concern | Conventional DEX | Spreadless |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pricing curve** | Constant product, curved everywhere | StableSwap invariant, flat near parity |
| **Slippage on size** | Grows steeply | Stays minimal while the pool is balanced |
| **Liquidity provision** | Two-sided, manual 50/50 split | Single-asset deposit |
| **Execution preview** | Often opaque | Rate, price impact, network fee, and minimum-received shown before signing |
| **Settlement** | Varies | Stellar finality in ~5 seconds, fees a fraction of a cent |

## Why Stellar

Stellar was designed for moving money, not for maximally-general computation. For
a stablecoin venue that means fast, cheap, deterministic settlement, native
issuance of regulated assets, and a smart-contract layer (Soroban) capable enough
to run the invariant on-chain while staying inexpensive to interact with.

## Transparency as a feature

Before you sign anything, Spreadless shows you the exchange rate, the price
impact, the estimated network fee, and the exact **minimum you will receive**,
the same floor that gets enforced on-chain (see
[Slippage & Safety](/docs/protocol/slippage-and-safety)). Nothing about the trade
economics is hidden until after the fact.
