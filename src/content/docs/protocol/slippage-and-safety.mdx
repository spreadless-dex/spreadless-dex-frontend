Every state-changing operation — swap, deposit, withdrawal — carries a **minimum
acceptable output that the contract enforces**. This is the core safety mechanism,
and it's worth understanding exactly.

## The two-phase pattern

For each write, the front-end:

1. **Simulates** the real call with the floor set to `0`. This returns the exact
   output the pool would give right now — the *quote*.
2. **Submits** the real call with the floor set to `quote − tolerance`. Your wallet
   signs this transaction. The contract recomputes against whatever the reserves
   are at inclusion time and either delivers ≥ the floor or **reverts**.

Because step 2 recomputes on-chain, any adverse movement between quote and
inclusion is caught. You are never silently filled below your floor.

## The floors, per operation

| Operation | Floor parameter | Baseline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `swap_exact_in` | `min_out` | The quoted output you accepted on screen |
| `deposit` | `min_lp_out` | The simulated LP-out at submit time |
| `withdraw_one_token` | `min_amount_out` | The simulated token-out at submit time |

## Why swaps use the on-screen quote as the baseline

For swaps, the tolerance is measured against **the exact quote that was on screen
when you hit swap** — not a fresh simulation taken a moment before submitting. A
fresh baseline would silently re-anchor to whatever the pool had already moved to,
letting real slippage through. Anchoring to your accepted quote means `min_out`
enforces precisely the number you agreed to.

<Callout kind="note" title="Tolerance in parts-per-million">
  Swap tolerance is expressed in **parts-per-million** (10,000 ppm = 1%), not just
  basis points. On tightly-pegged pairs, traders legitimately want floors below
  0.01% — finer than a basis point can express. Deposits and withdrawals use basis
  points (100 bps = 1%).
</Callout>

## Other safety surfaces

- **`paused()`** — a pool can be paused by its owner; the app reads this and the
  contract enforces it, so no trades settle against a paused pool.
- **Simulation errors surface as errors** — a failed simulation returns a
  `Result::Err`, which the app unwraps into a real, human-readable failure (e.g.
  *"Not enough USDx in your wallet for this amount."*) rather than submitting a
  doomed transaction.
- **Non-custodial throughout** — the contract only ever moves funds you authorise
  in the signed transaction; there is no approval that grants standing access.

## Risks the floor does *not* remove

The minimum-out floor protects your *execution*. It does not protect against:

- **A de-peg** of one of the pool's assets — the invariant will keep quoting near
  1:1 into a failing peg, accumulating the weak asset (see
  [Fees & Yield](/docs/concepts/fees-yield)).
- **Smart-contract risk** — as with any on-chain protocol.
- **A pool being paused** when you want to act.
